The Model Is Shared. The Harness Is Yours.

Andrew Hahm · May 2026 · 3 minute read

When a team gets curious about agentic AI, the default move is to hand everyone a powerful AI tool and call it a strategy. For engineers, that works. For a briefing team, it stalls inside a month.

Specialists do not write code, and they should not have to write prompts either. They run workflows. What they need is a system already loaded with the work they do every week: the briefing intake, the executive research, the recap, the customer history, the connections to CRM and calendar.

Two definitions worth holding

Agentic AI is what you get when you wrap a model in a harness: tools, memory, file access, the ability to hand work to other agents. The model does the reasoning. The harness does the work. Without the harness you have a chatbot. With it you have a worker.

Headless means the harness runs in the background. A briefing request lands, a calendar invite arrives, an executive replies, and a workflow fires. The system surfaces only what needs a person's judgment. It is not a thing you use. It is a thing you have configured.

Every company can rent the same model. That part is shared, and it gets cheaper every quarter. What nobody else has is your harness: your intake questions, your standard for a good executive bio, your account history. Put the engineering effort there, not into teaching specialists a new tool.

The stack, layer by layer

What makes it compound

A static harness rots. The gains come from skills that improve. Every time a skill misses, with a thin bio or a recap in the wrong tone, the correction goes back into the skill and not just into that one output. The first version of a skill gives you a decent draft. By the third, the corrections are small.

That is the return worth explaining to a CFO. Your specialists' first-draft quality climbs every quarter while their hours stay flat.

Do not give your specialists a coding tool. Give them a harness that already knows the job, and keep building the harness.
