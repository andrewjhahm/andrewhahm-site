Five Things Every Briefing Is Made Of.

Andrew Hahm · August 2026 · 3 minute read

"Bespoke at scale" sounds good in a strategy deck and means nothing until you can say what, exactly, gets customized. So here is the concrete version. Every executive briefing I have built comes down to five things, and each of them can be composed for one customer instead of pulled from a template.

Why we used to template

Before AI, composing all five for a single visit cost days of an engagement manager's time. So programs templated. A standard agenda, a standard deck, a standard welcome. Only the biggest accounts got a day built from scratch, and the template is the exact moment a briefing stops feeling built for that executive.

The mistake is to think the choice is between templates and chaos. The parts can be standard. The arrangement should not be.

Two altitudes

I hold two things apart. The program is the factory: the skills, the story library, the standards, the customer memory. It is configured once and it compounds. The briefing is what the factory puts out: one arrangement of those parts for this customer, these executives, this room.

What agentic AI changes is the cost of the arrangement. A system that can read the customer context and assemble the right research, stories, guardrails and experience in minutes makes a composed briefing cost about what a templated one used to. If an operator still has to hand-build each one over days, you have reinvented the expensive version, and nothing scales.

So the first job of the program is to make composition cheap. Everything else follows from that.

The one most programs skip

Room, material and experience get the attention because they are visible on the day. Guardrails get attention after the first mistake. The loop gets skipped, because by the time the customer's car pulls away the team is already preparing the next visit.

That is the most expensive omission in the function. What an executive says in a well-run briefing is some of the best customer intelligence a company ever gets. If it stays in the room, the briefing was an event. If it goes back to the business in a form people can use, it becomes an asset.

Template the parts. Compose the briefing.

Always forward
