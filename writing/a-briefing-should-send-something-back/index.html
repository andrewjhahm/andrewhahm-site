A Briefing Should Send Something Back.

Andrew Hahm · September 2026 · 3 minute read

Most briefing programs are built to send a story in one direction. The company prepares, the customer arrives, the story gets told well, everyone shakes hands. Then the room empties and almost everything the customer said leaves with them.

I think that is half a program. A briefing should tell the company's story on the way in and return customer intelligence to the business on the way out.

What a program is worth

When I map what a briefing program delivers to the business, it comes to five things. Most programs reach three. Strong ones add the fourth. Almost none have built the fifth.

Why the fifth one never got built

Everyone agrees with it in principle. In practice it has always lost to arithmetic. Capturing what was said, structuring it, and getting it to product, sales and marketing in a form they will read is hours of synthesis per briefing. The team that would do that work is already preparing the next visit. So the notes sit in a folder, and the best customer signal the company heard that month reaches nobody who could act on it.

The handoffs are lossy by default. Product hears a secondhand summary. Marketing hears nothing. The account team remembers what mattered to the account team.

What changes now

Synthesis is the thing agentic AI makes cheap. A system can take the notes and the transcript, pull out what each executive said about priorities, objections and timing, and route it. Product gets the feature signal. Sales gets the next steps and the attribution. Marketing gets the language customers used. The customer gets a follow-up that reflects what they said, not a generic thank-you.

A person still certifies what goes out. What a customer says in a briefing is said in confidence, and deciding what travels, and to whom, is a judgment call. I would not hand that to software.

But the economics have flipped. The loop used to be the first thing cut. It can now be a standard output of every visit, and it compounds. After a year you have a record of what your most important customers told you, in their words, that no survey or CRM field comes close to.

A program that only sends a story in is a cost center with good hospitality. A program that sends intelligence back is something the business would miss.

Always forward
