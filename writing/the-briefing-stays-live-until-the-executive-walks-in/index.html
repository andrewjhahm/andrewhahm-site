The Briefing Stays Live Until the Executive Walks In.

Andrew Hahm · July 2026 · 3 minute read

Briefing prep has always been waterfall. Weeks of planning, a locked pack, a big reveal. Nobody chose that because they liked it. High-fidelity work was slow to redo, so we front-loaded the plan and froze it early.

Everyone who has run a program knows what that costs. The account team calls four days out. The customer's priorities shifted, the CFO is now attending, and the topic they cared about in the planning call is no longer the topic. The pack is already baked. You patch a slide, brief the speakers in the hallway, and hope.

Two bad options

The alternative was to work the way software teams do: short cycles, rough drafts, fix as you go. That buys speed and gives up polish, and a rough draft is not something you put in front of an executive sponsor. So the choice was fidelity or iteration speed. Pick one.

When a finished pack regenerates in minutes

When a system can rebuild a near-final briefing pack in minutes, that tradeoff goes away. You produce a finished-grade version on day one. You put it in front of the account team while there is still time to be wrong. They tell you the CFO only cares about one thing, and an hour later there is a new finished-grade version built around that one thing.

I call it high-fidelity iteration. Waterfall's polish with agile's speed. Every pass is something you could walk into the room with, and you take many passes.

Late intel used to be a problem to absorb. Now it is the best input you get all week.

The medium follows the method

This is also why I have moved briefing material off frozen slide decks and onto living documents shared as a link. It is not an aesthetic preference. A deck emailed on Monday is stale by Wednesday, and nobody knows which copy is current. A living document regenerates, and the link always points at the latest version.

Some executives still want a printed leave-behind, and some companies live in slides. Fine. Export the deck from the live source the morning of the visit. You meet people where they are without freezing the work a week early.

One thing does not move. A named person certifies the facts in every version before it reaches an executive, which is the one rule I don't bend. Fast is only useful if it is also right.

Always forward
