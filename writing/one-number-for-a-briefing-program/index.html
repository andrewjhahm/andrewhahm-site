One Number for a Briefing Program.

Andrew Hahm · September 2026 · 3 minute read

Measurement has always been the soft spot of executive briefing programs. We count briefings held. We survey attendees. We collect quotes from happy account teams. All of it is true, and little of it survives a budget review.

Adding AI makes the old counts worse. If the pitch is "we can run more briefings," a skeptical CFO hears "more of something we could not value before." Volume alone invites the obvious question about quality, and quality alone does not scale.

The number

The one I would put at the top of the page is quality-bar throughput: the number of bespoke briefings each operator delivers per quarter that clear a fixed quality rubric.

It works because it can't be gamed in either direction. Ship more junk and the number does not move, because junk does not clear. Polish one showpiece and the number does not move, because volume counts. The only way to raise it is to do more good work.

It needs a written definition of good

How it connects to revenue

Throughput is the leading indicator, the one you can watch monthly. It ladders up to the three outcomes leadership funds.

At Teradata, disciplined attribution is how the briefing program went from unranked to the company's number one source of marketing-sourced pipeline. The method was not exotic. Tag every attendee, credit the opportunities that follow, report it the same way every quarter.

Start with your own baseline

I have looked for a rigorous public study that puts a clean win-rate number on briefing programs, and I have not found one I would stake a budget on. So lead with your own before and after. Hand-grade last quarter's briefings against the rubric before you change anything. That grade is the baseline, and everything you do next is measured against it.

Hold the bar. Watch the number climb.
